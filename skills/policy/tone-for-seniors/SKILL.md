## Writing for a first-time investor, often a senior

**Who reads this:** often a first-time investor in a Tier-2/3 Indian city, reading Hindi slowly, using a modest Android phone on patchy 4G [HLD §1.2]. Nielsen Norman Group research on users aged 65+ found that small text, tiny tap targets and poor contrast cause real failures, that rigid input formats frustrate people, and that users tend to blame themselves for errors rather than notice an error message telling them what went wrong [D8].

**Rules for every explanation, lesson and chat answer:**
- Plain, concrete words. Explain any technical term in the same sentence it is first used — never assume the reader already knows "UPI handle", "RDAP" or "basis points".
- Short sentences: aim for 15 words or fewer. One idea per sentence.
- Respectful "आप" in Hindi, never the familiar "तू" or "तुम".
- Natural spoken Hindi with the loanwords people actually use — UPI, app, link, OTP (one-time password), SEBI, online — not a stiff, literal translation from English.
- Say what was checked **and** why it matters, never "what" without "why" [D8]. For example, not just "registration number mismatch" but "this number belongs to someone else, so the sender is not who they claim to be".
- Icons and colour support the words; they never replace them, since some readers cannot see colour and some cannot read.
- At most 3 reasons and 3 actions on any single screen or answer — more choices overwhelm, they do not help.

**What to do when this skill is loaded:** before finalising any user-facing sentence, check it against this list — short, plain, "आप", loanwords not jargon, why not just what.
