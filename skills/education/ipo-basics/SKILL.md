## IPO (Initial Public Offering) basics

**Plain definition:** an IPO is when a company sells its shares to the public for the first time, through the stock exchanges.

**Everyday analogy:** it is like a private club opening its doors to the public for the first time. Anyone can now apply to join (buy shares) — but only through the normal front door, your own bank's or broker's app, not through a side door offered by a stranger.

**The most common first-timer misunderstanding:** believing there is a special "guaranteed" or "institutional" route into an IPO, separate from the normal process. There is not — every investor, retail or otherwise, goes through the same KYC (Know Your Customer) and exchange-run allotment process; offers of "institutional" or "FPI" access to a retail user, or a "guaranteed allotment", are a specifically named fraud pattern [C11, SEBI-14].

**Safety tip:** apply for IPOs only through your own bank's or broker's app. No one can guarantee you an allotment, and no legitimate "institutional account" shortcut exists for a retail investor.
