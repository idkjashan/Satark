## NAV (Net Asset Value)

**Plain definition:** NAV is the value of one mutual-fund unit — the fund's holdings minus its liabilities, divided by the number of units outstanding [C38].

**Everyday analogy:** it is like the price of one slice of a shared cake. The slice's price depends on how big the whole cake is and how many slices it is cut into — not on some arbitrary number printed on it.

**The most common first-timer misunderstanding:** thinking "a low-NAV fund is cheaper, so it's a better deal." It is not. Two funds holding the same assets can have very different NAVs simply because they were split into a different number of units; what actually matters is the *percentage change* in NAV over time, not its starting level [C38].

**Safety tip:** never choose a fund because its NAV "looks low" or "looks like it has room to grow" — that number tells you nothing about future returns. Compare a fund's track record, consistency and costs instead, and never trust a promised return attached to any NAV figure.
