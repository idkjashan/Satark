## Fees and the expense ratio

**Plain definition:** a fund's expense ratio is its yearly running cost, expressed as a percentage of its assets [C.6].

**Everyday analogy:** it is like a building's maintenance charge — it is deducted automatically every year, whether or not you ever see a bill for it.

**The most common first-timer misunderstanding:** thinking "no bill means no fee." It does not. The expense ratio is deducted directly from the fund's assets before the return reaches you, so you never see a separate invoice — but you are paying it every year regardless. Across all individual traders, transaction costs alone added up to about ₹25,000 crore a year [C.6, C1].

**Safety tip:** check a fund's expense ratio before investing, and compare it across similar funds. A lower cost compounds into a meaningfully larger balance over many years — small percentage differences matter a great deal over the long run.
