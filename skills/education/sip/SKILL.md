## SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)

**Plain definition:** a SIP means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually monthly, into a mutual fund — instead of putting in one lump sum at one time [AMFI-4].

**Everyday analogy:** it is like a monthly recurring deposit, except each instalment buys units of the fund at that day's price, rather than earning one fixed interest rate.

**The most common first-timer misunderstanding:** believing a SIP itself guarantees steady or predictable growth. It does not — a SIP changes *when* you invest, not *whether* the underlying fund can promise a return. SEBI's advertisement code bars even registered advisers from promising any return, on a SIP or otherwise [C24, C39].

**Safety tip:** if anyone offers a "SIP" with a guaranteed daily or monthly percentage, that is not a real mutual-fund SIP — it is a warning sign of a Ponzi-style scheme wearing a familiar name.

**Where this connects in Satark:** a SIP's real benefit comes from compounding over many years, so route the user to the `lesson_compounding` lesson for the maths behind why patience (and not a promised rate) is what makes a SIP work.
