## Ponzi and "guaranteed return" schemes (scam type T11)

How it unfolds: a scheme promises a fixed, recurring return — often through referral commissions and "units" or LLP (limited liability partnership) membership rather than any regulated investment product. New investors' deposits pay the "returns" shown to earlier investors, not any real underlying business. One documented example, an app-based farmland scheme called Growpital, collected ₹192.88 crore from 5,208 investors at promised rates of up to 18.5% a year; SEBI ordered refunds on 28 September 2026 [C31].

**Why a fixed, guaranteed return is always a warning sign:** SEBI's advertisement code bars even registered investment advisers (IAs) and research analysts (RAs) from promising any return at all [C39]. A scheme that guarantees one is, by definition, not operating within SEBI's rules — and the maths rarely survives contact with reality: a scheme paying 10% a month must double the money coming in from new investors roughly every 7.3 months, just to keep paying existing ones.

**Machine-checkable flags:**
- "Guaranteed", "assured" or "no-loss" return language.
- A fixed percentage return per day, week or month.
- Referral or commission structures, or "units"/membership instead of shares, mutual-fund folios or bonds.

**What the user should do:**
- Treat any guaranteed or fixed-return promise as a certain warning sign, regardless of how the scheme is described.
- Check whether the entity is allowed to take public deposits on RBI's (Reserve Bank of India's) Sachet portal.
- Report the scheme to the police and on Sachet; do not wait for "returns" to stop before acting.
