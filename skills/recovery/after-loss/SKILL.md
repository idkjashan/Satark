## After a loss: the golden-hour recovery steps

**Act within the golden hour** — the first 1–2 hours matter most. Call **1930** or file at **cybercrime.gov.in**, and keep the 14-digit acknowledgement number the portal gives [C33, I4C-1].

**Why speed matters:** the complaint enters CFCFRMS (the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System), which alerts every bank in the money's path. Those banks can place a hold on money still sitting in a receiving "mule" account, before it is withdrawn or moved again [C33].

**What happens next, by MHA's (Ministry of Home Affairs) 2 Jan 2026 procedure:** banks place a lien on the specific disputed amount, not a freeze on the whole account. Refunds under ₹50,000 can be released without a court order, once the money trail is verified [C33]. Victims claim released money back through the Money Restoration Module (`mrm-ncrp.mha.gov.in`), using the acknowledgement number [I4C-3].

**Where to report, depending on the case:**
- **1930 / cybercrime.gov.in** — any fraud where money moved.
- **Chakshu** (Sanchar Saathi) — a suspicious call or message where no money has been lost yet [C36].
- **SCORES** (SEBI complaints) — only against SEBI-registered entities or listed companies; it does not take complaints against unregistered fraudsters [C35, SEBI-6].
- **RBI's Sachet** — Ponzi or illegal deposit-taking schemes.

**Before uninstalling anything**, keep: chat screenshots with numbers and group links; the app's name, link or APK file; UPI (Unified Payments Interface) IDs, account numbers and IFSC; UTR (transaction reference) numbers; call logs; any "SEBI" documents received [C.4 in the research appendix].

**What the user should do:**
- Stop paying immediately — no further "tax" or "unlock" fee will ever release money already sent.
- Call the bank to flag the transaction and block further debits.
- Be alert to a second scam: a "recovery agent" who contacts the victim later, offering to retrieve the lost money for an upfront fee, is itself a scam [C37].
